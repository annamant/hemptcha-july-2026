# HempTcha  
### Brand & Business Brief  
*Confidential · For partners, creatives, buyers, and collaborators*

---

## One line

**Hemp leaf tea. Cold. In a can. Nothing to explain to anyone.**

---

## What it is

HempTcha is a ready-to-drink beverage brand built from organic European hemp leaf. Two drinks. Same leaf. Two completely different experiences.

Naturally caffeine-free. Non-alcoholic. Non-psychoactive. Food and drink — not a supplement, not CBD, not wellness theatre.

Regulatory is clean. The liquid tastes like green tea.

---

## The two drinks

This is not one product in two flavours. It is **two personalities under one brand** — different formats, different hours, different reasons to order.

### Bright Hour

**Iced hemp leaf tea · 250ml · Afternoon**

Brewed like a proper iced tea, chilled hard, canned. Named for the post-lunch dip — three in the afternoon, when the body runs low whether you’ve eaten or not.

| | |
|---|---|
| **In the glass** | Cold, clear, pale golden-green — like iced green tea |
| **Taste** | White peach and citrus first, cut grass behind, finish dry and clean — no sugar hanging around |
| **How you drink it** | Fast. Everyone does. |
| **Serve** | Tumbler. One large cube so it doesn’t water down. Lemon peel across the top. |
| **Bar line** | *“Bright Hour — iced hemp leaf, cold and dry.”* |
| **Need it meets** | Reset. Alert. Refined. Something sharp between lunch and evening. |

Bright Hour is the drink you finish before you’ve decided to. Light. Bright. No performance.

### Green Hour

**Stone-ground hemp leaf, ready to drink · 250ml · Evening**

Leaf stone-ground matcha-style, whisked, canned. Named for the hour Paris used to stop work and pour something green — eight in the evening. Sit down. It isn’t going anywhere.

| | |
|---|---|
| **In the glass** | Pale green-tea gold, faintly rounder; foam head after a hard shake |
| **Taste** | Softer and creamier through the middle than Bright Hour; long grassy finish that stays |
| **How you drink it** | Slowly. The point is the minute it holds. |
| **Serve** | Coupe. Shake the can. Pour cold and unhurried. Foam on top. Mini disposable whisk on the napkin beside the glass — not in the drink. *Give it three turns* if you want. |
| **Bar line** | *“Green Hour — stone-ground, shaken, in a coupe.”* |
| **Need it meets** | Reward. Unhurried. Cultured. Something to hold after dinner that isn’t another cocktail. |

Green Hour is the ritual. Bright Hour is the reset. Same brand. Different appointment with yourself.

### Why two — and only two

| | Bright Hour | Green Hour |
|---|---|---|
| **Psychological need** | Reset | Reward |
| **Speed** | Fast | Slow |
| **Self-image** | Alert, refined | Unhurried, cultured |
| **Bar occasion** | Afternoon / pre-dinner | Evening / post-dinner |

This is not flavour segmentation. It is **personality segmentation**.  
The gap between them *is* the brand. Do not add a middle SKU early — it would blur the story.

## Behavioural positioning

Most drinks are sold on what’s inside the can. HempTcha is sold on **what ordering it does for the person**.

### The real job

Nobody at a London bar at eight o’clock lacks hydration.  
What they lack is a **socially acceptable reason** to pause, hold something beautiful, and not drink alcohol — without performing virtue, disclosing a medical story, or becoming “the sober one.”

**HempTcha sells permission.**

Permission to pause.  
Permission not to drink.  
Permission to order something interesting without a speech.

The competitor is not kombucha.  
The competitor is the silence after *“Why aren’t you drinking?”*

### What other NA drinks signal — and what we don’t

| Typical NA signal | What it performs |
|---|---|
| “I’m healthy” | Virtue |
| “I’m sober” | Identity politics |
| “I’m adventurous” | Look at me |

HempTcha signals something rarer:

> **I’m the kind of person who discovers things before they need explaining.**

That is not rebellion. It is quiet authority.

- **Hemp** in the name → just enough intrigue  
- **Green tea taste** → never have to defend the choice  
- **The can** → not performing a home ritual for an audience  

Understated. Assured.

### What ordering it says about you

> **I have taste. I don’t perform it.**

Decoded:

| They are | What the room hears |
|---|---|
| Different, interesting | I choose with intention — not to provoke, because I notice things |
| Not flashy | My status isn’t loud labels or obvious luxury |
| Know their worth | I don’t need alcohol, CBD, or wellness to justify this |
| Sophisticated but understated | I’m comfortable where less is more |
| Don’t follow others | Not anti-trend — **post-trend** |

They didn’t reject the crowd to make a point.  
They left without announcing their departure.

### Identity lines

- **Nothing to explain to anyone.** *(the mantra — use everywhere people decide)*  
- For people who’d rather be interesting than obvious.  
- Not for everyone. Perfectly fine with that.  
- I don’t need your category. I have my own.

### Who this is not for

- Wellness optimisers  
- Sober-curious posters  
- Cannabis-curious teenagers  
- Anyone who wants to be asked *“what is that?”* every five minutes  

They might enjoy being asked once. They do not want to give a TED Talk at the table.

**Decision test for every asset:**  
*Would this person be embarrassed to be seen with this?*  
If yes — don’t ship it. Metrics don’t override taste.

### How behaviour shows up in the wild

**At the bar** — they order *Bright Hour* or *Green Hour*, not “the hemp drink.” Minimal elaboration unless someone worth talking to asks.

**In the group chat** — not *“Found this amazing healthy hemp drink!!!”*  
More: *“Had HempTcha at [bar]. Worth trying.”*  
Casual recommendation from someone whose taste is trusted. That is the retelling we want.

**On Instagram (if at all)** — can on a table. No face. No caption essay. Maybe the location. The product is the flex; they aren’t.

**Optimise for retelling, not only trial.** Trial happens once. Retelling travels.

### The bartender triangle

Bars don’t stock you because you’re organic. They stock you when pouring HempTcha makes **them** look interesting.

Design for three people at once:

1. **Buyer** — orders by Hour name  
2. **Bartender** — gets the credit for the pour  
3. **Brand** — gets the story retold  

Give trade a pour that looks like care, one line they can say aloud, and a story that fits in a sentence.

## The category (why this is new)

Hemp leaf tea already exists as **loose leaf**.  
HempTcha is the first serious attempt to treat hemp leaf as a **bar-ready cold drink**:

- Brewed or stone-ground  
- Chilled  
- Canned  
- Ordered by name  

It does not compete with CBD shots, adaptogen waters, or “calm” drinks.  
It opens a **new occasion category**: premium NA tea culture, sold like a back-bar pour, not a pharmacy product.

**Category claim (accurate):**  
Not “hemp tea never existed.”  
**Hemp leaf, cold, canned, for the glass — that is the invention.**

## Pricing positioning

### What we are selling (psychologically)

Not liquid. Not “hemp.” Not calories or calm.

We are selling:

| Asset | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Time** | An hour — Bright or Green |
| **Identity** | Taste without performance |
| **Permission** | No alcohol, no explanation |

None of those are cheap in London. Soft-drink pricing would put us on the wrong mental shelf.

### Stance

**Do not price like a soft drink.**  
**Price like a second cocktail without the hangover.**

| Wrong shelf | Right shelf |
|---|---|
| Soft drink / iced tea can | Premium NA / back-bar pour |
| Compared on £/ml | Chosen as an occasion |
| Impulse fridge | Ordered by name |

Cheap drinks are **compared**.  
Expensive drinks are **chosen**.

Reasonable pricing signals a reasonable product. HempTcha is not trying to be reasonable — it is trying to be **chosen**.

### Trade implication

At the bar, the guest should feel they ordered something with the weight of a proper pour — glassware, ritual, name — not a can of something from the soft-drinks list. Price must support that feeling for both guest and venue margin.

Exact numbers live in commercial planning. This brief locks the **position**: cocktail-adjacent NA premium, never soft-drink commodity.

## Packaging & design rules

1. **Signal tea, not hemp function.** No CBD. No “calm.” No leaf puns. No neon wellness.  
2. **Never “Dietary Supplement.”** This is a drink. Label it as one.  
3. **Brand leads. Hour names second.** Ingredient lines whisper.  
4. **Two can colours.** Bright Hour lighter (afternoon). Green Hour deeper (evening).  
5. **Design for the table at 9pm**, not only the shelf.  
6. **Small type = compliance. Large type = occasion.**

Visual: matte, restrained, European botanical. Calm authority — not look-at-me.

Holding the can should say: *this belongs in a good kitchen or on a good back bar — not in a hype cycle.*

## Go-to-market (first principles)

- Small first run. London. Curated bars and rooms.  
- Panel / waitlist before scale — at launch, scarcity is part of the product.  
- Trade needs a clear front door (regulatory + back-bar fit).  
- Consumer mystique and trade clarity can coexist — split the CTAs.  
- Two SKUs only for now. Protect the Bright / Green gap.
